How to choose

Shade Guide

Choosing colour online feels risky, but most decisions come down to two or three rules. This is what atelierR checks when matching a shade on a client — the same reasoning, written down so you can apply it yourself.

Cosmetics cannot be returned once opened, for hygiene reasons. That is why it is worth five minutes here before ordering.

Start here — your undertone

Undertone does not change with a tan, and it decides more than depth does. Look at the inside of your wrist in daylight:

  • Warm — veins look green, gold jewellery suits you, you tan easily. Choose beige, golden and coral tones.
  • Cool — veins look blue or purple, silver suits you, you burn before you tan. Choose rose, pink and plum tones.
  • Neutral — you cannot tell, and both metals look fine. Almost everything works; start with the shade described as “most chosen”.

Foundation

  1. Test along the jawline, never on the hand or the wrist.
  2. Look at it in daylight, near a window, without other makeup on.
  3. The right shade disappears. If you can see where it starts, it is wrong.
  4. Between two shades, take the deeper one — skin darkens slightly through the year in Singapore.

Most atelierR clients settle on a light-to-medium neutral beige. If you are ordering blind, that is the safest starting point.

Lipstick

  • For everyday wear, choose one or two steps deeper than your natural lip colour.
  • Matte finishes read deeper than they look in the tube. Choose one step lighter than you would in a glossy formula.
  • Sheer and glaze finishes are the most forgiving — they blend with your own lip tone rather than covering it.
  • If your eye makeup is strong, keep the lip quiet. If the lip is strong, keep the eye simple.

Blush

  • Warm coral and peach lift most skin tones in daylight.
  • Rose and mauve suit cool undertones and evening light.
  • A useful test: the colour your cheeks turn after a brisk walk is the colour that suits you.
  • Whatever you choose, tap the brush off before it touches your face.

Brow

  • Black or dark brown hair — choose one shade lighter than your hair. Matching exactly looks drawn on.
  • Lighter or coloured hair — match your hair as closely as you can.
  • Grey brown is the most-chosen shade among atelierR clients, and the most forgiving.

Understanding finishes

NaturalSatinMatte SheerLuminous
  • Natural — looks like skin, neither matte nor shiny.
  • Satin — a soft, low sheen. The most wearable for most people.
  • Matte — no shine, longest wear, best for evening.
  • Sheer — translucent colour that lets your own tone show through.
  • Luminous — reflects light softly, for a fresh or dewy look.
